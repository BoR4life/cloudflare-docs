The mark is not licensed for company use

Resolve this before the kit is applied to anything

The artwork is Us by Jean Jullien. It was not commissioned. Permission was granted for The Just Us Show, a monthly virtual reality gathering for nurses and teachers:

Jean is happy to let you use US, It would have to be limited to this project only, ie not used again over consecutive conferences etc. If you're ok with that, no fee for you.

The studio then asked how long the show would run. The reply proposed a year, or an initial six months, and raised use as an implied logo for the show. There is no further reply on record, so neither the term nor the logo use was agreed.

Company use differs on every axis: a different entity, an indefinite term, trademark use, and a derivative work, since a wordmark has been added beneath the artwork.

What is needed. A written licence from Studio Jean Jullien covering company identity, naming scope, territory, term, permitted media including print, garments and XR textures, and whether the wordmark lockup is permitted as a derivative. Expect a fee. If declined, commission a new mark — only the shape vocabulary below is mark-specific, so the system survives.

What the brand is for

Bundle of Rays sells XR and AI education to hospitals and universities. The people who decide are nurse educators, directors of nursing, clinical education leads, deans, ethics committees and procurement. They read documents for a living and can identify a template from the first screen.

The brand has one job: look like something a clinician built, not something a growth team shipped. Credibility first, and enough craft that it does not read as institutional beige.

The mark is the exception that earns the rest. It is warm, hand-drawn and faintly funny — the one part of the system that is not sober, which is what lets everything around it be sober without turning grey.

Colour

Ink and paper carry the brand. One accent does brand work. Red, amber and green are never brand colours — in a clinical setting they already encode early warning scores, triage categories and alarm states, and borrowing them for decoration is a category error in front of exactly the audience that notices.

That rule retired an earlier four-colour palette built from Jean Jullien's flat poster colour. Right for an illustrator's practice, wrong for this audience.

Typography

Archivo for display and interface, Source Serif 4 for body and long-form. Deliberately inverted from the usual serif display over sans body: a serif body reads as document rather than marketing page, which is the credibility move with this audience.

Archivo

Recognise deterioration earlier

Source Serif 4

400 and 600, measure 62 to 68 characters, leading 1.6

Graduate nurses arrive having practised on mannequins that do not deteriorate, do not argue, and do not have a family in the corridor. The gap between that and a real first shift is the gap this company exists to close.

Both faces are SIL OFL 1.1, and that is load-bearing rather than incidental: they can be embedded in Unity and Unreal builds without a licence problem. Commercial foundry licences routinely exclude embedding in an application binary, which is exactly what an XR module is. Do not substitute a paid face without reading the embedding clause.

Do not chase the hand-lettering. Never set type in a marker or faux-handwritten face. The wordmark is genuine hand-lettering by a serious illustrator, and an imitation beside it does not borrow its credibility — it lends the wordmark its own cheapness.

Texture and the ink field

The strongest device in the brand was already in use and had never been written down. A single full-bleed field of ink with a torn brush edge, the eyes knocked out to paper, and the wordmark beneath. No photograph, no accent, no explanation.

Use it for anything whose job is recognition rather than information: covers, opening slides, back pages, merchandise. The field bleeds off at least three edges. The edge is torn or brushed, never a straight cut or a rounded rectangle. The eyes knock out to paper, never to the accent. It carries the wordmark or a headline, never both plus body copy. If it needs body copy, it is the wrong layout.

Everything else

  • Paper grain sits over a full-bleed ground at 0.045 opacity and nowhere else. Drop it in-headset, where it becomes shimmer.
  • Gradients as decoration do not appear. A gradient must encode a scale or a progression.
  • One shadow value exists and it is for genuinely floating elements. Never on static cards.
  • No glassmorphism, glow, bevel, or stock imagery with circuit-board overlays.

The identity generates its own artwork

Influence: someform, Matthias Winckelmann and Helge Kiehl in Berlin. Their practice is identity built from logic, with tooling handed to the client so assets are produced in-house. Their rendered surface is simulated-photographic and would fight a brush-ink mark, so what is borrowed is the method rather than the surface.

Every module gets a cover derived from its name. Output is deterministic, so a code resolves to the same artwork permanently and the decision is never stored, briefed or remembered. The shape vocabulary is the mark's own and nothing else: the arch, the ray bundle, the pair of eyes.

Five archetypes keep output composed rather than random. Adding to or reordering that list reshuffles every existing seed, because the archetype is the first draw — lock it before covers go into production.
