# Marketing content

Public material has a tighter constraint than sales material: it reaches
regulators, competitors, procurement officers and clinicians who were not
in the room. Everything in `probity.md` applies twice over, and nothing
confidential appears at all.

## What public content is for

Not lead generation — there is no inbound volume yet and pretending
otherwise wastes effort. Public content does three jobs:

1. **Credibility that precedes the meeting.** A service executive who
   looks Brad up before a call should find a nurse and academic with a
   coherent position on AI in maternal education, not a vendor.
2. **Clinician sentiment.** Midwives and neonatal nurses talk. Sector
   sentiment shapes what a champion can get through governance.
3. **Recruitment of collaborators.** Academics, clinical champions and
   pilot sites find their way in through public argument far more often
   than through outreach.

## The strongest available angle

Brad's real position — a nurse who builds, arguing about how adults
actually learn — beats product marketing every time in this sector. The
recurring themes that land:

- Education that builds confidence versus AI that answers for you.
  Cognitive offloading is a genuine risk and most of the category is
  ignoring it.
- The postpartum cliff, and why front-loaded antenatal education fails
  the people who need it most.
- Governance as the differentiator in clinical AI — provenance, scope
  discipline, escalation that respects a parent's autonomy.
- Equity: who the default pregnancy app was never built for.
- The workforce argument — extending clinicians rather than replacing
  them, said by someone who has done the shifts.

## Formats

**LinkedIn.** One argument per post, opened with the claim rather than a
wind-up. Concrete, first-person, and willing to take a position someone
could disagree with. Australian English, no emoji strings, no hashtag
walls. A post that names a real tension in the sector outperforms a post
that announces news. End on a question that a practising clinician can
answer from their own experience — that is where the comments come from.

**One-pagers.** One audience per document. Decide first whether the
reader's budget is clinical, education or workforce, and write only to
that. Structure: the gap, what the Companion does, how it is governed,
what a phased partnership looks like, the ask. No pricing.

**Conference abstracts.** Follow the submitting body's structure exactly.
Lead with the education problem and the evaluation design, not the
technology. The pedagogy is the contribution — the AI is the method.

**Website and site copy.** Plain language, parent-facing where it speaks
to parents and service-facing where it speaks to services; never blend
the two on one page. Every claim must survive a clinical governance
reader.

## Never in public material

Pricing or tier structures. Unit economics, forecasts or funding gaps.
Named prospects, pilot targets or contacts. Investor identity. Any
settled-partnership or endorsement claim. Any therapeutic or diagnostic
verb. Screenshots containing real parent content.
