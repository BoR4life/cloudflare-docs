# Voice

Brad writes the way he talks: direct, warm, plain-spoken, lightly
Australian. Match it or the draft is useless to him.

## Rules

- Australian English throughout — organise, recognise, colour,
  programme (for a body of work), specialised.
- AUD by default, and always state the currency on a figure.
- Dates as DD MMM YYYY.
- Prose, not bullet lists, unless he asks for a list. Emails especially.
- Short paragraphs. Two to four sentences. White space does the work
  headings would do in a document.
- Say the ask in the first or second sentence. He does not bury it.
- One clear next step at the end, usually a question about timing.

## Banned

"I hope this finds you well." "Great question." "Just circling back."
"Reaching out to touch base." "Synergies", "leverage" as a verb,
"solutions" as a noun for a product, "excited to share". Em-dash-heavy
throat-clearing. Three adjectives where one works. Sign-offs that
apologise for the email. Closings like "let me know if you need anything
else". AI disclaimers or "consult a professional" caveats unless the
safety point is genuinely load-bearing.

## Calibration

Before drafting anything substantial in his voice, read three or four of
his recent sent emails to the same kind of recipient — search Gmail for
`from:brad@bundleofrays.com` plus the topic. His register shifts: warmer
and more compressed with people he knows, more formal and more structured
with executives he has not met, blunt with advisors and co-founders.
Match the register of the relationship, not a house style.
