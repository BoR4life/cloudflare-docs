# Probity gates

Hard rules. Every one of these exists because breaking it creates
regulatory, contractual or relationship exposure that is expensive to
undo. Check text against this list before it leaves the building —
emails, decks, posts, abstracts, grant copy, everything.

## 1. Education-only, non-diagnostic

Kindred Care educates. It does not diagnose, treat, triage, monitor or
manage. Drift toward therapeutic claims risks TGA classification as
software as a medical device, which would slow commercialisation
materially.

**Never write:** diagnoses, detects, screens for, monitors, manages,
treats, assesses risk, clinical decision support, triage, "flags
postnatal depression", "identifies complications", "reduces readmissions"
stated as a delivered outcome.

**Write instead:** educates, explains, prepares, builds confidence,
guides on when to seek care, routes back to the parent's own care team,
extends what clinicians already do.

Escalation language matters too. The Companion is autonomy-first: the
parent nominates her own care team, and when a red flag surfaces the
Companion prompts *her* to reach out. It never contacts anyone on her
behalf. Never imply the product alerts a clinician.

## 2. ACU naming

The correct formulation is **"an active, multi-pathway research and
commercialisation partnership exploration with ACU"**. Not "our partner
ACU", not "ACU-backed", not "in partnership with ACU" as a settled fact,
unless the specific agreement being described has actually been executed.

Every external document that names ACU is sighted by the ACU
Commercialisation Manager before release. Build that step into the plan
whenever you draft something ACU-naming — do not treat it as optional
because the deadline is tight.

Academic governance claims are fine and true: content is authored and
validated by ACU academics in midwifery, psychology and nursing. Say
that. It is stronger than a vague partnership claim anyway.

## 3. The institutional investor is never named

Internally or externally. Use "an institutional investor" or "the
programme". This holds even in notes, internal emails and working
documents.

## 4. Support is not endorsement

Several senior people and bodies have engaged positively. Engagement,
support and willingness to make introductions are **relationship access
and sector credibility** — not endorsement, not a committed partnership,
not a customer.

- A chief officer's willingness to make introductions: describe as
  engagement and access. Never as endorsement.
- Professional bodies that have given early support: describe as early
  support for the initiative. Never as accreditation, approval or
  partnership.
- A named individual who is enthusiastic: they are a champion, and inside
  their own organisation they carry weight. Outside it they carry none.
  Do not export their name as validation to a third party without asking
  them first.

## 5. Letters of intent are not revenue

LOIs and MOUs are non-revenue commitments to participate in pilot,
evaluation or reference-site activity. Never present them as pipeline
value, contracted revenue or customers. The correct framing is that the
footprint demonstrates demand without claiming revenue.

The founder's origin-company contracts in India are proof that the
*channel* works. They belong to the origin company, are not Kindred Care
revenue, and are never banked as such.

## 6. Maturity honesty

Kindred Care is at proof-of-concept maturity climbing toward validated
prototype. Architecture, content model and governance design are in
place; validated performance data is the work immediately ahead. Say
that. Do not describe the product as deployed, proven, in service or
evidenced until the evaluation exists.

The evidence-led sales motion — "here is the evaluation report, would you
like the same outcomes" — is the *future* motion. Today's motion is
co-development and early-adopter partnership. Pitch the honest one; it is
more attractive to the people who want to co-author the evidence anyway.

## 7. Confidentiality

The data room documents are Commercial-in-Confidence. Pricing, unit
economics, forecast figures, funding-gap positions and named pilot
targets do not go into public-facing material, LinkedIn posts, this
repository, or an email to a party outside an NDA. When an external party
needs commercial terms, that is a scoped conversation, not an attachment.

## 8. Cultural and community claims

Cultural adaptation for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander and CALD
communities requires community-controlled co-design and a real timeline.
Never claim cultural safety as delivered. Describe the co-design approach
and who holds it. Note that First Nations framing is included in some
funding tracks and deliberately excluded from others on ACU's
instruction — check which audience you are writing for before including
it.
