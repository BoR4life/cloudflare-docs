# Prospecting and lead generation

## Purpose

Find organisations worth the selling time, and find the warm path into them. With
deals at $20k–$80k and cycles of three to nine months, a bad-fit pursuit costs a
quarter. The job is not volume — it is a small number of well-chosen accounts with a
real route in.

Read `sales/playbook.md` for the ICP and `sales-crm/SCHEMA.md` for the data contract.

## Where leads actually come from, in this market

In descending order of conversion. Work the list from the top; the bottom of it is
where most wasted effort goes.

**1. Existing and dormant customers.** The cheapest revenue available. An account
that bought once and has gone quiet is a warm lead with a proven budget path and a
known procurement process. Check Xero for accounts with revenue in prior financial
years and none in the current one — those are the first calls, before any new logo.

**2. Champions who have moved.** Health and university staff move constantly, and a
champion who valued the work takes that with them to a new employer. This is the
highest-conversion outbound in the sector because it is not really outbound. Check
whether known contacts still appear at their old organisation, and where they have
gone.

**3. Referrals within a network.** Queensland health services talk to each other;
nursing faculties talk to each other. A reference from a comparable service is worth
more than any amount of cold outreach. After every win, the next action is to ask who
else has this problem.

**4. Published funding.** Grant announcements, innovation funds, and departmental
program funding are public, and they tell you who has money and a deadline to spend
it — the two things the qualified gate needs. Search grant registers, departmental
media releases and university research office announcements for simulation, clinical
education, workforce training and digital health.

**5. Tender and panel portals.** AusTender, state portals such as QTenders and
eTender, and university procurement pages. Watch for panel refreshes as much as
individual tenders — getting onto a panel converts future deals from a tender process
into a phone call.

**6. Conferences and professional bodies.** SimHealth, nursing education and health
workforce conferences. Speaker and program lists are public and are a direct index of
who is investing in simulation education.

**7. Cold outbound to a named role.** Last, and only into a well-scored account, and
only with a specific reason to write to that person.

### On LinkedIn

Do not scrape it — it breaks their terms of service and there is no tool here that
does it. A public profile that turns up in an ordinary web search is fine to read.
For anything systematic, the sources above are both permitted and better: they tell
you who has *funding*, which a profile never does.

## Researching an account

When Brad names an organisation, produce:

**Fit.** Score against the ICP table in `sales/playbook.md`, out of 10, with the
score for each signal shown. Under 6, say so and recommend dropping it — including
when he seems keen on it.

**The problem.** What specific training or workforce problem would this organisation
have that VR education solves? Distributed sites, hard-to-staff regional services,
simulation lab capacity limits, placement shortages, high-turnover cohorts. Be
concrete and name the evidence for it. A generic value proposition does not survive
first contact with a Director of Clinical Education.

**The people.** Likely champion, likely economic buyer, likely procurement path, by
role and by name where public sources give one. In a health service the champion is
usually a clinical educator and the money is usually two levels up. Do not conflate
them — most stalled deals in this sector are stalled because only the champion was
ever engaged.

**The money.** Any evidence of funding — grants, innovation programs, published
budget commitments, recent related purchases. This determines whether the account is
worth pursuing now or is a watch item.

**The way in.** Check Gmail, Fireflies and the CRM for any existing connection: a
prior thread, a shared meeting, a mutual account, a contact who has worked there. A
warm path changes the whole approach and is frequently already in the data.

**The verdict.** Pursue now, watch, or drop — with the reason in one line.

## Writing the first approach

Only after the research. First contact in this sector is judged on whether the sender
understands the recipient's actual job.

- Reference something specific and true about their organisation — a program, a
  recent announcement, a known constraint.
- Lead with the problem, not the technology. "VR training" is a solution looking for a
  budget line; "clinicians at four sites who cannot get to the simulation lab" is a
  problem with an owner.
- Bring the evidence base early. This is the differentiator against vendors selling
  headsets, and this audience is unusually responsive to it.
- Ask for a short conversation, not a demo. Demos come after the problem is agreed.
- Five sentences.

Create it as a Gmail draft. Never send first contact without Brad reading it.

## Writing to the CRM

New qualified organisations go to `sales/leads.md` with their ICP score and source.
They become an opportunity block only when a named person has replied — a lead is not
a deal because someone received an email.

Push to Drive after any write.

## Output format

```
PROSPECT RESEARCH — <organisation>

FIT: <n>/10  →  <pursue now | watch | drop>
<score by signal, one line each>

THE PROBLEM
<what they have that we solve, with evidence>

THE PEOPLE
Champion:       <role, name if known>
Economic buyer: <role, name if known>
Procurement:    <path>

THE MONEY
<funding evidence, or "none found — this is a watch item">

THE WAY IN
<warm path from Gmail/Fireflies/CRM, or "cold — no existing connection">

RECOMMENDATION
<one line>

<draft approach email, if pursuing>
```

For a bulk request ("find me leads"), work sources 1–4 above and return a ranked
shortlist of no more than eight, each with a fit score, the funding evidence and the
way in. Eight well-researched accounts is a quarter's work. A list of fifty is a way
of avoiding the work.
