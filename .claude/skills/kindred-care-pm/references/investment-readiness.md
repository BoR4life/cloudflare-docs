# Investment readiness — Kindred Care

Order of operations: **entity → evidence → non-dilutive → equity.** Skipping a step
does not speed the raise up, it just moves the delay later and makes it more expensive.

## Step 0 — the blocker that outranks everything

**Is Kindred Care an incorporated company with the 70/30 on a cap table and the IP
assigned in?**

If it is a contractual arrangement between Bundle of Rays and ACU rather than shares
in a company, external capital has nothing to buy. Every hour spent on a deck before
this is resolved is an hour spent on a document that cannot be actioned. IRL is capped
at 1 until it is answered.

Related questions that must be answered in the same piece of work:

- Where is the 70/30 documented, and is it equity, revenue share, or an MOU?
- Who owns the code, prompts, brand, and transcript data today — legally, in writing?
- Do any ACU staff or student contributions create a university IP claim? Universities
  ordinarily assert rights over staff-created IP; that has to be checked and cleared,
  not assumed away.
- Does ACU's 30% dilute with an external round, or is it anti-dilutive? An
  unaddressed answer here kills term sheets late, which is the worst time.
- Is Hector's contribution documented, with IP assigned and equity or compensation
  settled?

These are lawyer questions. The agent's job is to keep them on the board with owners
and dates and refuse to let the raise track advance past them — not to answer them.

## The Australian funding stack — non-dilutive first

Work these before equity. They change the size and price of any round, and several
require an incorporated Australian company, which is another reason Step 0 comes first.

| Source | What it is | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|
| **R&D Tax Incentive** | Refundable offset (43.5% for eligible companies under the turnover threshold) on eligible R&D | Often the largest single non-dilutive line for a company at this stage. Requires an Australian R&D entity, eligible experimental activities, and **contemporaneous records** — software claims are scrutinised, so the record-keeping has to start now, not at claim time |
| **Industry Growth Program** | Commonwealth advisory and matched grants for innovative SMEs in National Reconstruction Fund priority areas | Successor to Accelerating Commercialisation; check current priority-area fit and round status |
| **MRFF / NHMRC pathways** | Health and medical research funding | Only if the product carries a genuine health-outcomes research question — and ACU is the natural applicant, which has JV implications |
| **CRC-P** | Industry–research collaboration grants | Structurally well suited to a university JV; long lead times |
| **State innovation grants** | Varies by jurisdiction | Depends where the entity is based — currently UNKNOWN |
| **ACU internal** | Commercialisation office, seed schemes, in-kind staff time, facilities | Ask what the 30% partner is actually contributing, in writing |
| **ESIC status** | Early Stage Innovation Company — qualifying investors get a 20% tax offset and CGT concessions | A material sweetener for Australian angels. Assess against the current gateway/principles tests with an adviser and, if it qualifies, say so early — it changes who will take the meeting |

Verify every scheme's current rules and rounds against primary sources at the time of
writing. Program names, thresholds and eligibility in this sector change year to year;
never quote a figure from this file into an application without checking it.

## Milestone-to-raise map

Investors fund the *next* set of de-risking milestones, not the vision. Build the ask
by working backwards:

1. Name the readiness triple you are raising **to** — e.g. TRL 7 / CRL 6 / IRL 5.
2. List the milestones that get there, each with a cost and a date.
3. Sum them, add the runway to reach the milestone *after* the raise plus a buffer.
4. That is the number. Use of funds is then simply that list — which is the format
   every investor asks for and most founders reverse-engineer badly.

Keep this map in `kindred-care/program-board.md`. It is the same milestone list the
weekly review already tracks — the raise narrative and the program board should never
be two different documents.

## Data room index

Assemble in this order; publish the index with gaps visible rather than hiding them —
an honest gap list reads as competence, a discovered gap reads as concealment.

**Corporate** — certificate of registration, constitution, cap table, JV/shareholder
agreements, board minutes, founder and contractor agreements with IP assignment.
**IP** — ownership schedule for code, prompts, brand, data; any university IP
position; trademark status; third-party and open-source licences; model provider terms.
**Product** — architecture summary, security posture, roadmap, current TRL with evidence.
**Safety and compliance** — crisis/escalation design, incident log and process, privacy
impact assessment, consent artefacts, the written regulatory classification position.
**Market** — ICP, pipeline, pilot agreements, LOIs, pricing, competitor analysis.
**Evidence** — pilot results with pre-declared criteria, publications, ethics approvals.
**Financials** — historical accounts, model, burn and runway, R&D Tax Incentive
position, grant status, use of funds.
**Team** — bios, roles, time commitment, key-person risks and how they are covered.

## Diligence question bank — the ones that will actually be asked

Prepare written answers before the first meeting. These are the ones specific to this
venture; the generic ones are easy.

1. "It's a JV with a university — what exactly am I buying, and does ACU's 30% dilute?"
2. "Who owns the IP, and has the university signed that off?"
3. "The CEO runs another company, is doing a PhD, and holds a fellowship overseas. How
   much time does Kindred Care get, and what happens to it when the thesis is due?"
4. "Your evidence base comes from research the founder runs. How is that independent?"
5. "Is this a medical device? Who told you it isn't, and in writing?"
6. "You're talking to vulnerable people. What happens when someone discloses suicidal
   ideation at 2am?"
7. "Where does the conversation data go, who consented to what, and can you delete it?"
8. "Your product depends on a third-party model. What happens if pricing, terms or
   behaviour changes, and can you switch?"
9. "Show me a customer who pays."
10. "What's the founder's own research say about the risks of this product category?" —
    the warmth trap. Answer this one proactively. Naming your own failure mode before
    an investor finds it in a published paper converts a liability into evidence of
    unusual seriousness.

Question 3 is the one founders under-prepare and investors weight heavily. The honest
answer — with a stated time allocation, a named second person, and what changes
post-raise — is far stronger than a deflection.

## Investor update discipline — start before the raise

Write a monthly update from now, even with no investors. It builds the habit, creates
a track record of hitting stated milestones, and is the single most effective warm-up
for a raise: by the time you ask, the recipient has watched you deliver for six months.

Format — one page, same order every month:

```
# Kindred Care — <Month YYYY>

**TRL n / CRL n / IRL n** · Runway: <n> months · Cash: AUD <x>

**Highlights** — three bullets, evidenced.
**Lowlights** — at least one, honest. An update with no lowlights is not believed.
**Metrics** — the same three to five numbers every month, never a changing set.
**Against last month's asks** — what was asked, what happened.
**Asks** — two or three specific, actionable requests. Names, not categories.
```

Never change the metric set to flatter a month. Changing what you measure is the
loudest possible signal that the numbers went the wrong way.
