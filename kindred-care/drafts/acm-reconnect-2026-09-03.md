Hi Mia, Gabby — and adding Kelley, who I met back in April.

This thread stopped in July on a time Russell offered and I never picked up. That was mine
to close and I didn't. Apologies.

Rather than ask for another slot, here is the thing I actually owed the College — and a
draft of one of the three ideas you put on the table in April.

**The engagement pathway.** When Gabby, Kelley and I met on 22 April, ACM raised three
possibilities: clinical oversight of what we're building, short courses on AI for midwives
with your education team, and space at the conference. I took an action to come back with a
considered six-to-twelve-month pathway. Here it is, plainly:

1. *Now* — the College sights what we're building at whatever depth is useful. No
   expectation of endorsement, and nothing said publicly either way.
2. *Months 1–3* — a short-course module on recognising postnatal deterioration, developed
   with your education team. Draft outline below. **It is clinical content first — it
   stands on its own whether or not it ever mentions AI or us.**
3. *Months 3–6* — only if the College sees value, a conversation about what clinical
   oversight could look like, what it would not be, and what ACM would need to be
   comfortable.
4. *Months 6–12* — conference presence, and any evaluation findings shared with the
   membership whichever way they fall.

**Where we are.** Bundle of Rays is working with Australian Catholic University on Kindred
Care — a midwifery- and nurse-led education Companion for women from 20 weeks gestation to
twelve months postnatal. It is education and signposting only: it does not triage, interpret
symptoms or diagnose, and clinical escalation is deterministic and cannot be overridden.
Russell and Bill from ACU are on this thread and are the better people to ask about anything
on the university side.

**The attached draft.** *The Ten Complications of Postnatal Care* — the complications
carrying the greatest burden of maternal death and serious morbidity in the first six weeks,
ordered by consequence rather than frequency, with risk windows and sourced figures. I wrote
it as a nurse first.

Its argument is one I suspect will be familiar to you: most postnatal education is organised
around what is common, which produces staff who are confident with engorgement and uncertain
about a rising shock index. And when you read the risk windows in sequence against a
24-to-48-hour discharge, almost the entire peak-risk period sits outside direct clinical
observation. Two-thirds of women readmitted with postpartum hypertension had no antenatal
diagnosis to flag them.

It is offered to the College to use, adapt or discard, with no condition attached.

**Draft module — Recognising deterioration after discharge**

| Session | Content | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Frequency versus consequence; reading a risk window; the discharge gap | 45 min |
| 2 | The time-critical four — haemorrhage, sepsis, VTE, hypertensive disorders. Compensated physiology and the early signals | 90 min |
| 3 | The under-recognised four — perinatal mental illness, mastitis, iron deficiency, wound morbidity | 90 min |
| 4 | The unasked two — bladder and bowel dysfunction, cardiac complications | 60 min |
| 5 | The neonatal counterpart, and the dyad as one follow-up problem | 45 min |
| 6 | *Optional and separable* — where supervised AI does and does not belong in postnatal follow-up | 45 min |

Session 6 is deliberately last and deliberately removable. If the College would rather the
module carried no AI content at all, that is a perfectly good outcome and the module is
complete without it.

**The ask, and it's small.** Thirty minutes with whoever in the education team should see
this — or tell me it isn't useful, which I'll take as the honest answer it is. Either way
you'll hear from me on a schedule from here rather than in bursts.

Cheers,
Brad
