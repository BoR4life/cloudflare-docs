# Kindred Care — Exit Strategy

**Draft 0.1 · 30 Aug 2026 · UCAC pack deliverable · Confidential — ACU & BoR**

*UCAC requires an exit strategy; nothing existed. This draft is written in the pack's
register — modest, evidenced, no unicorn language — and is built to survive the question
"how do ACU and Innovation Victoria get their money back?" It needs Brad's edit and
Russell's sight before it goes anywhere.*

---

## The position in one paragraph

Kindred Care is being built as a durable operating company, not engineered for a quick
sale — and that is precisely what makes it acquirable. The realistic exit window is
**FY30–FY32**, after the Victorian pilot evidence is published, revenue is repeating
across multiple services, and the moats (governed corpus, evidence pipeline, channel)
have compounded for three to four years. The most probable exit is a **trade sale to a
strategic acquirer**; the credible fallback is a **profitable hold** with shareholder
returns from operating cashflow — a fallback most software ventures cannot claim, and
which materially strengthens negotiating position in any sale.

## Who buys a company like this

Ranked by probability, with the reason each would pay:

1. **Australian health-tech and patient-engagement incumbents** (hospital software and
   engagement platforms). They own health-service relationships and generic products;
   Kindred Care gives them a governed, clinically credentialed maternal vertical they
   cannot build without a university partnership and years of corpus governance. This is
   the classic "buy the moat" acquisition.
2. **Private health insurers.** Maternity is the single most switching-sensitive product
   line in Australian private health insurance; insurers already run maternity programs
   in-house (e.g. fund-paid models). An insurer acquisition converts Kindred Care from a
   licence business to a member-retention asset. The insurer channel hypothesis is a
   standing Phase-3 memo in the data room.
3. **Catholic and mission-aligned health groups.** The sector consolidates around
   mission fit; a governed maternal education Companion inside a Catholic university JV
   is an unusually clean cultural match. (Cabrini's own Eve product makes this sector
   simultaneously competitor and plausible consolidator.)
4. **International maternal digital-health entrants** buying an Australian regulatory
   footprint, an English-language governed corpus, and published evidence.
5. **Growth equity / secondary sale** — a partial exit allowing ACU or early holders to
   realise while the company continues; realistic from FY31 if trading matches plan.

An IPO is **not** part of this strategy at any modelled scale, and no document in the
pack should imply one.

## What each shareholder needs from an exit — and how this design delivers it

**ACU.** Return on its co-investment; continuity of the research relationship; no
reputational tail risk. The IP architecture already provides for this: ACU retains
**research and teaching licences** over the knowledge base, evaluation frameworks and
persona design regardless of who owns the company, and academic publications remain
ACU's. An acquirer buys the company; the university keeps the scholarship. ACU's exit
proceeds flow through its shareholding under the shareholders' agreement to be settled
at incorporation.

**Innovation Victoria (BVUIP).** Return via its shareholding, with follow-on
participation already contemplated at Series A; and continuity of Victorian benefit.
Exit negotiations should carry standard Victorian-benefit undertakings (operations,
employment and data remaining in Victoria for a defined period) — these are common in
state co-investment exits and cost little against price.

**Bundle of Rays.** Return on background IP licensed in plus founding equity. Because
the platform is **licensed, not assigned**, a trade sale prices either a licence
continuation or a buy-out of the platform licence — both are negotiable assets and the
structure should be priced both ways when the time comes.

## What makes the price — the value drivers to build deliberately

Exit value here is not a revenue multiple story at this scale; it is an asset story.
The four assets an acquirer cannot quickly replicate, in the order they compound:

1. **The governed corpus and its audit trail** — versioned, ACU-signed, growing monthly
   from the POC onward. By FY30 this is 4+ years deep.
2. **Published evidence** — pilot evaluations and PhD-grade outcomes research with
   citations. Each publication is permanent and transferable.
3. **Contracted relationships** — multi-service licences on standard terms, the ACU
   partnership, and the escalation/governance infrastructure health services have
   already assured.
4. **Regulatory position** — the education-only, non-SaMD posture, held consistently in
   writing since inception. An acquirer inherits a clean file, not a remediation.

Milestones that move the price step-wise: published pilot evaluation (FY28) → three-plus
services on standard terms (FY29) → statewide or insurer channel agreement (FY30) →
international reference site (FY30+).

## Comparable transactions

A comparable-transaction schedule (Australian and international maternal/women's digital
health acquisitions, patient-engagement platform consolidation) is a listed dependency
for v1.0, to be compiled with the corporate adviser closer to the event. This draft
deliberately quotes no multiples: at pre-revenue, quoted multiples signal naivety rather
than ambition, and UCAC will read the discipline as intended.

## Governance of an exit

Exit decisions sit with the KC-Co board under the shareholders' agreement to be executed
at incorporation, which should include: drag/tag provisions; ACU and Innovation Victoria
consent rights appropriate to their holdings; the Victorian-benefit undertakings above;
and the publication-protection protocol surviving any change of control (no acquirer may
suppress in-train academic publications — this protects ACU and, candidly, protects the
evidence asset that gives the company its price).
