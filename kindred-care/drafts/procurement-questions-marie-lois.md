# Questions for Marie and Lois — procurement and budget

**30 Aug 2026.** Brad is asking both. These are ordered by how much the answer changes the
pricing model — question 1 in each set is the one worth the meeting on its own.

Do not ask all of these in one go. Ask the top three, and keep the rest for the follow-up.

---

## For Marie — the commercial and procurement read

**1. Which budget line does this come out of, and who owns it?**
The most important question in the whole set. Workforce and education? Quality and safety?
Perinatal mental health? A programme grant? Everything downstream — the price, the pitch,
who we meet, what evidence they need — is set by the answer, and we are currently guessing.
If the honest answer is *"there isn't a line for this"*, that is the finding, and it means
the first revenue is programme money rather than a health service's operating budget.

**2. Does the procurement threshold apply to annual value or to total contract value over
the term?** Almost always total, in which case a three-year agreement at $18,000 a year is a
$54,000 procurement and pricing under a threshold achieves nothing. This single answer
decides whether the entry-price strategy is real. Ask it plainly.

**3. What is the direct-engagement threshold at the services you know, and who signs at
that level?** A cost-centre manager, a department head, or does anything above zero route to
procurement? The number matters less than the identity of the signatory.

**4. Is there a state panel or standing offer we could get onto — HealthShare Victoria or
similar?** If a panel arrangement exists for digital health or education products, getting
listed bypasses the per-service threshold question entirely and is worth more than any
pricing decision we could make. Highest-leverage item on this page after question 1.

**5. Is a small pilot contracted separately, or does procurement assess the intended full
deployment value from the outset?** If the latter, the pilot is not a way in and we should
stop treating it as one.

**6. What does a maternity service actually pay for comparable digital products today, and
is any of it visible?** Contract disclosure registers, if they exist, would tell us more
about the real ceiling than any amount of modelling.

## For Lois — the service and sector read

**1. Can a group of regional services buy together?** Is there a Victorian regional
alliance, network or shared-services structure that contracts on behalf of several small
maternity units? **This may be the single most valuable thing to learn.** 33 of Victoria's
52 providers are regional and small, and at two of our three unit-cost estimates a small
service is loss-making at any price it would accept. If they can buy as a network, the
regional market opens. If they cannot, the individual small service may not be a viable
customer at all and the model has to change.

**2. Who in a maternity service actually holds an education or workforce budget?** Director
of Midwifery? Nurse Educator? A workforce or professional-development unit? Named role, not
department.

**3. Is there precedent for a maternity service buying an education or digital learning
product, and roughly what did it cost?** Even one data point is worth more than the whole
pricing model. If the honest answer is that services rarely buy anything like this, that is
also the finding, and it points at grant and programme funding for longer than we have
assumed.

**4. What does Taskforce implementation funding or Safer Care Victoria actually fund — and
is it applied for by a service, or allocated centrally?** This determines whether the state
licence idea is a real route or wishful thinking, and it is the same question as who we
should be talking to.

**5. Would a service expect to pay for this at all, or expect it to arrive as a state
programme?** Uncomfortable question, best answer we can get. If the sector expectation is
that maternal education tooling arrives free from the department, per-service selling is the
wrong motion regardless of price.
