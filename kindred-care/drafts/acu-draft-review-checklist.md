# Awaiting ACU's draft — the holding line, and how we assess what arrives

**Stood up 30 Aug 2026.** Brad's direction: *"we are awaiting for documents from ACU.
No need to get too specific. ACU will have a draft approach. Once we receive that we can
review."*

Two consequences. First, we stop proposing an ownership structure — including the IP
Plan's version, which the second-pass review recommended adopting. That recommendation is
withdrawn. Second, the five contradictory statements still have to go, because waiting is
not the same as being inconsistent. The fix is to make the pack **neutral**, not to pick a
winner.

> the Heads of Agreement, currently in preparation with ACU."**

### What stays stated, because it is separable and true

Ownership of the JV and ownership of the background IP are different questions. The second
is settled and evidenced, and must not get swept up in the neutralisation:

> **"Bundle of Rays Pty Ltd owns the underlying multi-tenant platform as background IP.
> It is licensed to the joint venture, not assigned."**

Evidenced by the Business Plan §5 and the `bor-iridia` build history. The Product Overview
already states it correctly. Do not soften this one — it is the single most valuable thing
BoR brings to the table, and going quiet on it while ownership is open would be a costly
silence.

**Net effect:** five positions become one holding line plus one confirmed IP statement,
with BoR's preferred structure preserved as a negotiating position rather than published
as a fact. Nothing is conceded and nothing contradicts.

## Part 2 — Assessing ACU's draft when it lands

Read it against these before responding. The first four are structural; getting them wrong
is expensive and slow to reverse.

### The four that matter most

1. **Is the background IP licensed or assigned?** Licensed in is the only acceptable
   answer. Assignment of the platform to the JV transfers BoR's whole asset for a minority
   of a company that does not exist yet. If the draft assigns, that is the single point to
   push back on and everything else can wait.

2. **Where do improvements to licensed IP flow?** The IP Plan's own term — improvements
   flow back to the licensor and re-license to the JV — is defensible and common, but it
   cuts both ways and **BV will scrutinise it**: improvements to BoR's platform funded with
   BVUIP money would accrue to BoR rather than to the company BV invested in. Check which
   direction ACU has drafted it, and have the answer ready either way.

3. **Does the split reflect contribution, and is contribution defined?** A percentage
   without a contribution schedule behind it is a number to argue about later. What counts
   — cash, the platform, clinical governance, academic capacity, the $650k obligation? If
   ACU's draft names a split with no schedule, ask for the schedule before discussing the
   number.

4. **Is ACU's $650,000 cash obligation acknowledged in the document?** It is arithmetic
   from the 50/50 BVUIP structure and it has been open for 60 days with no identified
   source. A Heads of Agreement that structures ownership while staying silent on the cash
   is half a document.

### The eight to check in the pass after

5. Does the draft distinguish KC-S / KC-U / KC-Co, or collapse them? The pathway matters
   for when obligations bite.
6. Board composition and reserved matters — who can block what.
7. Treatment of the PhD firewall. The five-question check must survive the structure.
8. Does it name the institutional investor? **It must not.** Probity lock.
9. Foreground IP — does KC-Co own all of it, including anything ACU staff create?
10. Student and clinical placement IP — ACU has standard positions here that may reach
    further than expected.
11. Exit and drag/tag provisions, and whether ACU can block a trade sale.
12. What happens to the licence if the JV fails — does the platform come back clean?

### Standing rule

**No response to ACU's draft goes out without Russell sighting it, and nothing is agreed
in a meeting.** The draft gets read against this checklist, the assessment gets written
down, and the response is drafted for Brad to send. Same autonomy boundary as everything
else: the agent drafts, Brad sends.

**Legal review is not optional on this document.** The checklist above is a commercial
read, not advice. Before signature, this needs a lawyer who has done university JV and
background-IP licensing — the improvements clause and the assignment question in
particular.
