# What Elena is — plain-language statement for mothers. v0.1

**30 Aug 2026. DRAFT for Lois's clinical review and legal review before use.** Three
purposes in one short document: **APP 5 notification**, the consent-flow explanation, and
ISO/IEC 42001 transparency evidence.

**Written for a woman who may be sleep-deprived, anxious and holding a baby.** Short
sentences. No legal throat-clearing. No "we take your privacy seriously". Target: readable
in under two minutes, at roughly a Year 8 reading level.

**Placement:** shown before consent, not buried in terms. Reachable at any time from the
Companion. Available in the languages the service enrols in — *if it is only in English, the
equity claim is weaker than stated.*

---

## The statement

### Elena is a computer program

Elena is not a person. She is an artificial intelligence — a computer program that reads
what you write and writes back.

She was built by Bundle of Rays with midwives and nurses, and the information she draws on
has been checked by clinicians. But she is software, and she can be wrong.

### What Elena is for

Elena is here to **explain things**. Pregnancy, birth, feeding, your body, your baby,
sleep, what to expect and when.

### What Elena will not do

**Elena will not tell you what is wrong with you, or what to do about a symptom.** She
cannot examine you, she cannot see your baby, and she does not know your medical history.

She will not diagnose, and she will not decide whether something is serious. That is your
midwife's, doctor's or nurse's job, and it is not something she can take over.

### If something is urgent

**If you are worried about yourself or your baby right now, contact your midwife or doctor,
or call 000.**

Do not wait for Elena. If you tell Elena something that sounds urgent, she will say so and
show you who to contact — but she cannot call anyone for you, and she cannot send help.

### Who can see what you write

- **Your conversation is stored securely** and is linked to you.
- **A small number of trained staff can read what you write** where there is a safety
  concern — so that someone can follow up if you need help. ⚠ *[Name the roles and the
  circumstances precisely once §3.3 is final.]*
- **Your health service ⚠ [can / cannot] see your conversations.** *[This must be answered
  before the pilot. She will assume the answer is yes unless told otherwise.]*
- **Your conversations are not used to advertise to you, and are not sold.**
- Anonymous, grouped information about how Elena is used helps improve her. **What you write
  is not part of that.**

### If we ever need to use conversations for research

We will ask you first, separately, and you can say no. **Saying no changes nothing about
your care or your access to Elena.**

### Your choices

- You can stop using Elena at any time.
- You can ask us what we hold about you.
- You can ask us to delete it. ⚠ *[Safety records may need to be kept — say exactly which,
  and why, once the legal ruling is made. She must know this before she consents, not
  after.]*
- You can complain to us, and if we do not fix it, to the Office of the Australian
  Information Commissioner.

**Contact:** ⚠ *[named person, real email, response time]*

## The four ⚠ items, and why they matter

None of these are drafting problems. Each is a decision that has not been made, and the
statement cannot be published while they are open:

1. **Which roles can read her free text, in which circumstances.** The §3.3 matrix work has
   narrowed this to three escalation roles — restate in her language.
2. **Whether her health service can see her conversations.** ⚠ **The most important unanswered
   question on this page.** She is enrolled by her service and will assume they can. If they
   can, that changes what she discloses — particularly about mental health and family
   violence. If they cannot, that is a strong trust signal that should be said clearly.
3. **The erasure position, including the safety-record exception.** Blocked on the legal
   ruling in `retention-and-erasure-design-note.md`.
4. **A named contact.** Not an inbox. There is currently no Privacy Officer.

## Why this document earns its place

It closes the autonomy gap in the impact assessment (§2.6), it is the APP 5 notification, it
is 42001 transparency evidence, and it is a pilot prerequisite.

It also does something the pack does not currently do anywhere: **it states the product's
limits to the person the limits protect.** The education-only boundary is enforced
architecturally and described to investors and health services — but never to the mother. She
is the one relying on it.
