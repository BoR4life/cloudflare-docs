## How we engage — the ladder, and the one thing to fix in it

The five-step ladder in the Customer Discovery Summary is sound and should not be replaced:

**Intro meeting (60 min) → Co-design workshop (half day) → Letter of Intent → Pilot
agreement → Procurement**

Two principles worth keeping verbatim: *"No commitment is asked at a higher rung than has
been earned at a lower rung"* and *"each rung produces a written artefact for the data
room."* That is genuinely good practice and most ventures do not have it.

**What is missing: nothing enters the ladder without a named route and a declared interest.**
Add a rung zero.

### Rung 0 — Routing and declaration

Before any approach, record: who is introducing, what their interest is, and which pathway
this counts as (ACU network / Safer Care Victoria / Chief Midwife / BoR relationship /
inbound). A prospect introduced by a shareholder is not disqualified — it is *declared*, and
the declaration goes on the COI register before the meeting, not after.

This single addition resolves the Peninsula question and prevents the next one.

### What to say at Step 1 — the opening that works

Evidence from the trail says the strongest opening is not the product. It is **Keppel's
gap**, put as a question:

> *"When mums leave your service, what happens in the first six weeks that you can't see?"*

Then listen. The Customer Discovery Summary already says it for Cloudnine — *"listen first,
do not pitch"* — and it applies everywhere. Kindred Care is introduced as the answer to what
they describe, not as a product with features.

**Frame on workforce and equity, never on patient education** — that has no budget line.
And lead with what the CDIO wants to hear early: **no EMR integration, no IT project, zero
added staff load.** That is the incumbent's main advantage turned into a virtue, and it
removes the largest single objection before it is raised.

### The value exchange — say it out loud at Step 1

Sites give 4–8 hours of senior time, a non-binding LOI on letterhead, and cohort
recruitment support. They receive de-risked AI they can sanction, **named academic
co-authorship on peer-reviewed publications**, and preferential pricing locked for three
years.

Co-authorship is the underused half. For a Director of Midwifery, a named publication is
career currency that no discount matches.

### Enrolment is part of the offer, not an implementation detail

The retention evidence is unambiguous: clinic enrolment with a tangible package retains a
median 167 days; self-enrolment retains zero. **The welcome pack — box, journal, magnet,
already designed in the IP plan — is part of what the site is being offered**, because it is
what makes their pilot work. Say so at Step 2.

## The honest summary

The venture has **more early-adopter substance than its documents claim** — a confirmed
in-kind clinical partner, two signed letters of support, a three-pillar Queensland
partnership model, an invitation to a maternity leadership meeting, and four credible
international pathways.

And it has **less Victorian substance than its documents claim** — no site past Step 1, two
prospects idle for four months, a channel strategy resting on relationships the named
channel-holder says are in another state, and the one person who could unlock all of it
waiting on a follow-up email that Russell requested three weeks ago.

Both of those need to be true in the pack at the same time. The Queensland strength is the
proof the model works; the Victorian gap is the work the POC funds. Told that way, it is a
coherent and honest position. Told the way the documents currently tell it — Victorian
channel strong, no signed artefacts — it is wrong in both directions at once.
