## 1. The strategic position, stated plainly

Kindred Care is a **midwifery- and nurse-led, education-only conversational Companion**
for mothers across the perinatal period, built on ACU-validated content, sold to health
services and enrolled through them.

Three assets are real and rare in combination:

- **A working product well past prototype.** 97 of 148 MVP stories shipped, four services
  running, safety controls proven by planted mutation rather than assertion.
- **A university partner with the channel.** ACU Midwifery under Prof. Lois McKellar is
  the most credible route into Victorian maternity services in the country.
- **An evidence engine.** A PhD producing peer-reviewed work on exactly this product
  class, including its failure modes.

One asset is missing and everything waits on it: **an incorporated entity with the 70/30
on a cap table and the IP assigned in.** Kindred Care Pty Ltd does not yet exist.

## 2. The competitive position — Eve, answered properly

Russell's instruction of 26 Aug is the live requirement: *"Competitor analysis needs to be
done…. we need to show we know industry space and can evidence why KC is superior to any
aligned existing/pipeline products."* Nobody owns it. It is the first thing this strategy
closes.

### What Eve is

Built by **Cabrini Health (Victoria)**, launched **2020**, national and Victorian *Not
for Profit Solution of the Year* 2021, delivered through **Medicity (Cabrini Technology
Group)**. Used by **50,000+ women**. Deployed across Victorian services including
**Northern Health, Eastern Health, Western Health and St John of God Berwick**.
Commercial model: **free to the mother, the hospital subscribes** — identical to Kindred
Care's intended model.

Its feature set: antenatal visit notes and test results visible in-app, direct chat with
a midwife, a moderated peer forum, evidence-based content in text and video, and tracking
tools (maternal health, mental health, birth planner, growth, contraction timer).

### The honest read

**Brad's instinct on 26 Aug is the right frame and should be the pack's line:** *"They
launched in 2020. That is 6yrs ago… You are reporting that clinical teams have
challenges."* Six years of incumbency and the clinicians Lois speaks to describe it as
*"a difficult app to use and not specifically about education."* That is not a market
that has been won. That is a market that has been **occupied but not served**.

But the pack must not rest on that alone, because Eve holds three things Kindred Care
does not:

| Eve's genuine advantage | Kindred Care's answer |
|---|---|
| Integration with the antenatal record — visit notes, test results | Kindred Care has no EMR integration. **Do not claim parity.** Position as complementary to the record, not a replacement for it |
| A human midwife in the chat loop | Kindred Care has 24/7 availability that a human roster cannot match, plus a staffed escalation queue for the moments that need a person |
| Incumbency, an award, and a Catholic health-service parent | ACU is the Catholic university. This is the reason to treat Eve as a *partner option*, not only a rival |

### Where Kindred Care wins, defensibly

1. **Different job.** Eve is record access plus tools plus asynchronous human chat.
   Kindred Care is **education delivered conversationally, on demand, at the moment of
   need**. A mother at 2am with a question about feeding does not want a portal.
2. **Content governance.** ACU academic sign-off, an approval inbox, and an immutable
   corpus. Nobody else in this market has a university's midwifery faculty as the content
   authority.
3. **Provenance.** The documented failure of generic conversational AI in maternal
   information is *omission of clinically important counselling content and no verifiable
   references* (Horgan et al., *AJOG MFM* 2024). RAG provenance answers exactly that.
4. **Evidence.** A publishable evaluation pipeline, which Eve — a not-for-profit product
   team — has not built.

### The decision nobody has made

**Compete, partner, or be acquired.** Cabrini is a Catholic health service; ACU is a
Catholic university; the Companion is education-only and Eve is record-and-tools. On
paper these are complements, not substitutes. Kindred Care inside Eve's install base is a
faster route to 50,000 mothers than displacing it from four health services.

**Recommendation:** take a compete-first, partner-open position. Build the pack on
displacement-by-superiority, and separately ask Russell to test the Cabrini relationship
through ACU's own Catholic-sector network before UCAC. Being the party that already
considered it is worth more than being asked about it.

### Also to be mapped

Russell also names the *"Vic midwifery pipeline thing"*. If that refers to Marie's own
COVID-era app, it is both a competitor entry and a conflict item — see §6.

## 9. Pricing

**Price on annual birth-volume bands.** The forecast already does this and it is right:
births per year is a number every maternity service reports, it is stable and
forecastable, it maps to their own cost language, and it does not punish the service when
the Companion is used well.

**Never per-active-user.** It turns engagement — which is Kindred Care's job — into the
customer's financial risk, caps revenue exactly when the product succeeds, and invites
the buyer to litigate retention figures during negotiation.

**External anchor.** A maternity app with library content and asynchronous midwife chat
lists on the NHS G-Cloud marketplace at **£55,000 per licence per year**. Convert at a
current rate and put it in the Pricing Model as the comparator — a defensible external
anchor is worth more at UCAC than an internally derived number.

**Pilot one is a fixed fee including the evaluation**, sized under the service's
direct-engagement procurement threshold. Never a discount off list.

**Equity is an uplift, not a discount.** The SCHHS/Wishlist arrangement already works
this way. Discounting the regional site says the regional cohort is worth less, which is
the opposite of the case being made to BV.

**One correction to close before UCAC.** The mother survey uses **Van Westendorp**. That
is a consumer willingness-to-pay instrument and it **cannot set the B2B licence price**. A
health service pays a fraction of the cost it avoids — midwife time, avoidable triage
contacts, unplanned presentations. Keep Van Westendorp for the B2C tier; anchor B2B to
avoided cost.

## 10. Pilot design — what makes the evaluation saleable

The pilot exists to produce a result a Victorian maternity service will buy on. That
constrains it more than the research question does.

1. **Enrol through the service, at a clinical moment, with something tangible.** This is
   the highest-leverage design decision in the whole strategy. Never a QR code on a
   poster.
2. **Design an enrolment artefact.** The 167-day cohort had a physical self-examination
   package. Kindred Care has an enrolment code. Something must make enrolment an event —
   a midwife-delivered onboarding, a personalised perinatal plan generated on the spot.
3. **Select for need.** A postpartum chatbot RCT reported 91% satisfaction, 74% two-week
   use — and a limited effect, because the sample did not screen positive at baseline.
   High satisfaction with no effect size is unsellable and unpublishable at once.
4. **Pre-declare success criteria, denominator and retention definition**, against the
   **43% pooled dropout** benchmark for app-based interventions.
5. **Stratify by education, parity and digital confidence, pre-specified.** Adherence runs
   *higher* with education and tech confidence and *lower* among mothers who already have
   children. BV is funding equity reach. Unstratified, the evaluation will show a
   regressive result and undercut the case.
6. **Measure enactment, not just usage.** Only 38% of studies in the field do. Measuring
   it puts Kindred Care above the field's own reporting standard and aligns with the
   DEFINE reporting-guideline work.
