### One thing worth saying plainly about the engineering

The evidence standard on this board — *"a check that cannot go red is documentation,
not evidence"*, controls proven by planted mutation rather than assertion, QA refusing
a fix because the control passes when the code producing it is deleted — is stronger
than most funded companies in this sector can show. Two examples that will matter in
diligence: the cache-key collision where every unowned account shared one key was
proven by demonstrating one principal reading another's private transcript pre-fix and
neither seeing the other post-fix; and the fixture-leak closure ruled on a run-over-run
delta of zero rather than a flattering total.

**That is a data room asset, not just good practice.** A health service's security
review and an investor's technical diligence will both ask how you know the safety
controls work. Very few answers are as good as "here is the mutation that proves it."
It belongs in the data room index as evidence in its own right.

## Gate review: CRL

**Assessed: CRL 2. Result on CRL 3: FAIL.**

The commercial *model* is built and it is a good one — multi-tenant by institution,
plans, sponsorships, seat caps, enrolment codes, usage ledger, entitlement. B2B2C is
already in the schema, which fits the LHD/HHS direction exactly.

What is missing is everything outside the code: no named district, no executive sponsor,
no budget line, no pricing hypothesis, no competitor list, no assurance pack. CRL 3
requires a named segment, named organisations and a route to reach them. None is
evidenced.

**The domain is now clear and it sharpens the target considerably.** The ten
maternal-issue codes, EDD capture with life stage derived on read, and support tickets
containing free text mothers wrote about themselves put this squarely in
**maternal / perinatal**. In LHD/HHS terms that means maternity services, child and
family health, and perinatal mental health — not "aged care", and not a general
wellbeing product. That is a far more specific and more reachable buyer than the market
track was carrying yesterday, and it maps directly onto ACU's nursing and midwifery
placement relationships.

## Timeline risk

**The gap between MVP complete and the next district funding window is the single
largest scheduling risk on the program.**

At ~193 hours of loop time across two or three parallel tracks, MVP complete plausibly
lands in **late September or October 2026**. The next Feb–Apr proposal window for
FY2027–28 district funding is roughly **five months after that**.

Five months of a finished product with nowhere to go is not a disaster — it is exactly
the time required to close the entity question, obtain HREC approval, settle the
regulatory position, build the assurance pack, pick a beachhead and draft a funded-pilot
application. But that only happens if those items are worked deliberately **now**,
in parallel with the build, rather than started when the build finishes.

Two downstream consequences worth holding:

- **Analytics & evals is the evidence engine, and it is at 0 of 12.** Its own
  description names "Director, HREC and platform views". The epic that produces the
  evidence a district's governance committee reads, and the views HREC needs, is the
  one that has not started — and it is downstream of FIX-DB-001, currently in the loop.
  For an evidence-led go-to-market this is not backlog, it is the product's commercial
  surface. It deserves to be a track, not a leftover.
- **A 9–18 month sales cycle from a standing start in Feb 2027** puts first district
  revenue somewhere in **late 2027 to mid 2028**. That is the number the raise has to be
  sized against, and it is why the round must fund ~24 months.

## Firewall check — triggered

Two of the five questions in `acu-jv-and-phd-firewall.md` fire on this board.

**Q1, data collection.** Transcripts exist as a product capability (VS-013, VS-019) and
are the data source for PhD Paper 5. HREC approval is listed as an **open out-of-loop
blocker**. Therefore: any transcripts collected from real users before HREC coverage is
in place, under consent wording that does not cover research use, **cannot be used for
Paper 5** — and consent cannot be retrofitted after collection. Given the product is
weeks from MVP complete and enrolment is already live in dev, this needs settling before
the first consented user, not before the first pilot.

**Q5, ACU's position.** ACU holds a governance role *inside the running product* —
a `content_sme_acu` role, an approval inbox, and corpus documents that cannot reach the
live index without approval. That is a **fifth hat** on top of owner, ethics approver,
supervisor's employer and channel. It carries an operational dependency the others do
not: if the ACU approval seat is unstaffed or slow, the knowledge pipeline stalls in
production. Who staffs it, and against what turnaround, is now an open question.

and the only one that is free to start.
