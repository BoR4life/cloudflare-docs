## Fifth finding: the competitor analysis has a hole where the incumbent should be

You named **Eve** and you were right to. What the public record shows: a maternity app
built by **Medicity, part of Cabrini Technology Group**, used by **50,000+ women**, and —
this is the part that matters — deployed across **Victorian maternity services** including
Northern Health, Eastern Health, Western Health and St John of God Berwick. Its commercial
model is *"free of charge to all maternity patients attending a hospital with an Eve
subscription"*.

That is **the same buyer, the same state, the same B2B hospital-subscription model** as the
Kindred Care beachhead. GTM §7.1's competitor table names static content sites, generic
LLMs, generic patient-engagement portals, international consumer apps and insurer
programs. It does not name Eve. UCAC and Breakthrough Victoria will.

The honest read cuts both ways and the pack is stronger for saying so:

- **Eve's real advantages:** integration with antenatal visit notes and test results,
  direct chat with a human midwife, a moderated peer forum, and incumbency inside the
  exact services you are targeting. Deep EMR-adjacent integration is something Kindred
  Care does not have.
- **Kindred Care's real advantages:** a 24/7 governed conversational agent rather than
  content plus asynchronous human chat; academic content provenance with ACU sign-off; a
  research evidence pipeline; and a safety architecture proven by planted mutation.

One more thing worth deciding deliberately rather than being asked: **Cabrini is a
Catholic health service and ACU is a Catholic university.** Eve is as plausibly a
partner, channel or acquirer as a competitor, and that is a live strategic option, not a
throwaway. Decide it before someone at UCAC raises it.

## The recommended approach

### 1. The register is the plan. Do not build a second one.

You already have the best programme artefact in this venture. The agent's job is to read
it, report the delta, and hold the layer above it — decisions, risks, gate verdicts, and
consistency across the pack. When the board and the register disagree, the register wins.
I have written that rule into the skill.

### 2. Run the checkpoints against the critical path, not against everything

87 tasks is too many to review twice a week. **31 are on the critical path.** Monday reads
the critical path and the two ACU blockers; Thursday checks whether Monday's decision
moved. Anything off the critical path is reported only when it slips onto it.

### 3. Between checkpoints, the agent works the document backlog in gate order

These are the ones the agent can genuinely draft, in the order their gates fall:

| # | Artefact | Why now | Register task |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **UCAC pack gap map** — every checklist item, artefact, owner, due date, status, blocker, on one page | Nothing else can be sequenced without it | P6-04 / P6-14 |
| 2 | **Eve competitor brief + revised GTM §7.1** | It is the first question an assessor asks | P1-08 |
| 3 | **Exit strategy** | UCAC-required, open dependency, nobody has started, pure document work | P1-14 |

| 7 | **Consistency sweep** — TRL, language, funding quantum across every pack document | Cheapest credibility you will ever buy | P6-15 |
| 8 | **Business plan v3** consolidating the above | Due 25 Sep | P1-16 |

### 4. Give Russell a weekly one-pager, not a request

The two blockers are his. The most effective thing a programme manager does with an
external owner is remove every excuse for delay: state the item, its age in days, what
specifically is needed, and a draft of it wherever a draft is possible. That one-pager
goes out with the Monday review.

### 5. Fix the connector gap in the scheduled reviews

The Monday and Thursday Routines currently carry no connectors, so the sessions they fire
cannot read Drive — they cannot see the register, which is now the whole point. **Recreate
both Routines from the claude.ai Routines UI with Google Drive attached.** Until then the
scheduled reviews run blind and you will be pasting.
