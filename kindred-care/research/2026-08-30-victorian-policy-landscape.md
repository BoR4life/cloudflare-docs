## The headline

**The Victorian Government has committed to nine recommendations to reform maternity care,
is appointing the state's first Chief Midwife to implement them, and is building a
state-funded consumer platform called My Maternity Journey for completion in 2026.**

None of this appears in any Kindred Care document. It is happening in the beachhead market,
funded by the same government whose investment arm the venture is asking for money.

It is mostly very good news. But it must be in the pack before an assessor raises it, because
an Innovation Victoria analyst will know all of it.

## What has actually happened

| What | Detail | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Victorian Maternity Taskforce** | Established 2024 by Safer Care Victoria; initial focus rural and regional; addresses workforce, service delivery, culturally safe care | Report *"Victorian Maternity Taskforce Report – rural and regional"* released by Health Minister Mary-Anne Thomas, early November |
| **Nine recommendations** | Workforce recruitment, regional service delivery, culturally safe care; strategies to improve access, choice and outcomes | Government committed to implement; some accepted immediately |
| **Chief Midwife** | Statewide clinical leadership for maternity and newborn services **for the first time in Victoria**; will *lead implementation of the taskforce recommendations* and advise government | Named as the first step |
| **My Maternity Journey** | Consumer-facing website; *"a central platform of resources and supports to better inform and empower pregnant women"*; modelled on My Surgical Journey; co-designed with consumers, clinicians and sector partners; VACCHO partnership for First Nations content; embeds respectful care and perinatal mental health | **On track for completion in 2026** |
| **Respectful Maternity and Newborn Care Framework** | Issued by Safer Care Victoria in response to the Taskforce | Released 6 January 2026 |
| **Midwifery Continuity of Care (MCoC)** | Scale-up identified as a policy direction | Sector priority |

## Why this is mostly very good news

**1. The state has just validated Kindred Care's problem statement in its own words.**

The GTM currently argues the problem from first principles and from the Chief Midwife's
private comments. It can now argue it from **government-committed policy**. The Taskforce's
own themes — workforce recruitment pressure, rural and regional service delivery, culturally
safe care, access and choice — are the four things the Companion addresses. That is the
difference between "we believe there is a gap" and "the Victorian Government has committed
to nine recommendations about this gap."

For a Victorian co-investment case, that is the strongest external validation available and
it is free.

**2. Alignment targets now exist and are nameable.** The pack can state alignment to the
nine recommendations, the Respectful Maternity and Newborn Care Framework, and the MCoC
direction. Concrete policy alignment beats generic "digital health strategy" language in
every assessment rubric.

**3. Brad already has the relationship that matters most — and the venture is not using it.**

The Chief Midwife is the most senior new role in Victorian maternity and exists specifically
to implement the reform agenda. The programme register records *"Victorian Chief Midwife
(done)"* under primary market analysis, and Russell's Overview deck proposes building the
problem slide *"borrowing partly from the comments of Chief Midwife."*

**The person appointed to implement Victoria's maternity reform has already discussed the
problem with Brad.** That is a stronger opening than any pilot site, and nothing in the
plan follows up on it.

## The one that needs a position: My Maternity Journey

A state-funded, free, consumer-facing maternal information platform, launching into the
beachhead market in the same year. Three readings, and the venture needs to have chosen one
before September 16.

**Reading A — it is a competitor.** Free, government-branded, trusted, universal. If a
service can point every woman to a state resource at no cost, why licence a Companion?

**Reading B — it is not the same product, and the distinction is one Kindred Care has
already articulated.** My Maternity Journey is described as *a central platform of
resources*, *a trusted evidence-based guide*. That is structured content — the category the
Product Overview already identifies as failing: *"Maternal education today is fragmented and
front-loaded."* A guide answers the questions you know to ask, in the order someone else
chose. The Companion answers the question actually being asked, at 3am, in her words, at her
stage. Raising Children Network, Tresillian and Karitane are all excellent and none of them
closed this gap; a very good state website will not either.

**Reading C — and this is the one worth pursuing — it is the channel.** A state content
platform needs an interface; a governed conversational layer needs authoritative content.
My Maternity Journey will carry VACCHO-developed cultural content, respectful-care framing
and perinatal mental health material, co-designed and state-endorsed. Kindred Care exists to
deliver exactly that kind of content conversationally, with provenance, at the moment of
need. **"The conversational layer over Victoria's own maternal content"** is a materially
stronger proposition to Innovation Victoria than "a product that competes with the thing
your government just built."

**Recommended position:** B publicly, C strategically. Name My Maternity Journey in the
competitive section, explain the content-versus-conversation distinction honestly, and open
the channel conversation through Safer Care Victoria — which is already on the market track
as an unworked route via Elisa McDonald.

**The clock.** If the state completes My Maternity Journey in 2026 and then adds a
conversational layer to it, the window narrows sharply. That is a real competitive risk with
a date on it, and it argues for engaging Safer Care Victoria sooner rather than after UCAC.

## A correction the pack needs

The Premier's office states **Victoria has 52 maternity health service providers, including
33 in regional areas.**

The Business Plan says *"Victorian maternity services (75+ facilities, ~80,000 births
annually)."*

**52 against 75+.** The government's number is lower, and it is the number an Innovation
Victoria assessor will use. Correct the Business Plan to the state's own count — and note
that the smaller denominator makes the Victorian market more concentrated, not less
attractive: 33 regional providers is a defined, reachable list, and regional is exactly
where the Taskforce is focused and where the equity case lives.
