**Draft 0.1 · 03 Sep 2026 · for Lois McKellar, Bill Kelly and the R&D Workshop group**
**Confidential — ACU & BoR**

*Companion document to `drafts/pilot-protocol-v0.1.md`. This is the study that comes
BEFORE the pilot. The pilot asks "does it work?" This asks "are we building the right
thing, for whom, and what would make them use it?"*

**Nothing in this protocol is enacted until the ethics pathway is determined and, where
required, HREC approval is in place.**

---

## 1. Why this study exists

The single largest evidence hole in the Kindred Care pack is that **every claim about what
mothers want is asserted, not evidenced.** The corpus taxonomy, the modality doctrine
(text-first, video for demonstration only), the 16-month cohort window, the equity case,
and the feature roadmap all rest on inference from literature and clinical opinion. None
rests on a single Australian parent having been asked.

This study closes that hole and simultaneously fills four other gaps:

| Gap it fills | Where the output lands |
|---|---|
| No consumer voice in content governance | Advisory committee — the consumer/lived-experience seat |
| Feature roadmap prioritised by build convenience | Hector's backlog ordering |
| Modality doctrine (text-first) is a reasoned assertion | `references/interface-doctrine.md` — evidenced or corrected |
| Equity claim (CALD, First Nations, regional) is structural, not empirical | Business Plan, Innovation Victoria narrative |
| Cohort window 20wk–12mo is a product definition, not a finding | `canonical-facts.md` |

**What it is NOT.** This is CRL evidence, not TRL evidence. It does not advance TRL 4 —
that still requires the gold-standard eval set. Do not let the two be conflated in any
document or at UCAC.

## 2. The structural recommendation: run it as two tracks

**This is the most important decision in the document.**

A university HREC study cannot produce results before the UCAC window. Realistic low-risk
timeline: instrument Sept → submission early Oct → approval late Nov → fielding delayed by
the December–January period → results **March 2027 at the earliest**.

So split it:

### Track A — Commercial product discovery (BoR-run)
- **Purpose:** inform the roadmap, the modality decision (D-23) and the pack. Internal.
- **Run by:** Bundle of Rays. **No ACU investigators.** Not for publication.
- **Timeline:** instrument in 2 weeks, field 3 weeks, results **mid-October**.
- **Status of evidence:** indicative. Never cited as research. Never described as a study.
- **Its second job:** it field-tests the instrument that Track B then uses.

### Track B — The research study (ACU-led)
- **Purpose:** publishable, citable, generalisable. The asset that survives scrutiny.
- **Run by:** ACU Midwifery with BoR. HREC-approved.
- **Timeline:** results Q1 2027 — which aligns with when BVIC money actually lands.
- **Its second job:** it is the first program under the new advisory committee, so it
  exercises the governance we are standing up.

**The determination that Track A does not require HREC review is NOT ours to make.** Under
the National Statement, involvement of ACU staff as investigators, use of ACU resources, or
any intent to publish will pull it into scope. **Lois must confirm the pathway for Track A
in writing before a single response is collected.** If the answer is that it needs review,
Track A collapses into Track B and we lose the October data — that is an acceptable
outcome, but it must be a known one, not a discovered one.

## 3. The design trap this protocol is built to avoid

**Do not ask parents what features they want.**

Stated-preference feature surveys produce a wish list that correlates poorly with use.
Every item polls positively, nothing discriminates, and the output is a roadmap that
justifies whatever was already planned. It is the most common way a discovery study
produces the appearance of evidence and none of the substance.

The instrument is therefore built on four principles:

1. **Ask about events, not features.** "In the last four weeks, was there a moment you
   needed an answer and could not get one?" — then what it was, when, what you did, and
   what happened. Recalled behaviour beats hypothetical preference.
2. **Force trade-offs.** A MaxDiff / best-worst core makes respondents give something up.
   This is what produces a rank order that can actually drive a backlog.
3. **Measure the current workaround.** What they do now is the real competitor, and it is
   almost always Google, a Facebook group, or calling their mother. Cost, delay and
   confidence of the current workaround is the value case.
4. **Capture the 2am case explicitly.** The modality doctrine stands or falls on whether
   moments of highest need are the moments video is least usable. Ask directly: time of
   day, hands free or not, others asleep, what device was within reach.

## 4. Research questions

**Primary**
- RQ1. What information and support needs do parents experience across pregnancy from
  20 weeks to 12 months postnatal, and how do those needs vary by stage?
- RQ2. What do parents currently do when a need arises, and what does that cost them in
  time, confidence and unresolved worry?
- RQ3. Which forms of help do parents prioritise when forced to trade off, and does that
  rank order differ by stage, geography, language background, or first vs subsequent baby?

**Secondary**
- RQ4. What modality do parents prefer, in which circumstances — and specifically, what
  is usable at the moment of highest need?
- RQ5. What are parents' expectations, concerns and conditions of acceptance regarding an
  AI Companion supervised by midwives — including what would cause them to stop using it?
- RQ6. What are partners'/fathers' own needs, and are they a distinct segment or informants
  on the mother's experience?

## 5. Population, sampling and the partners question

**Eligible:** adults, currently ≥20 weeks pregnant or with a child ≤12 months, resident in
Australia. Partners of an eligible person are eligible in their own right.

**The partners decision is a product scope decision, not a research one.** The current
product cohort is mothers. Including fathers and partners here is valuable but must be
deliberate, because it flows straight into pricing — the tier caps are per *enrolled
mother*, and a partner seat is either free (a cost we have not modelled) or a second
enrolment (a pricing change).

**Recommendation:** include partners as a distinct stratum at ~25% of sample, framed as a
**segment feasibility question**, and pre-commit that the answer is allowed to be "not a
separate segment". Do not include them and then quietly treat the result as a mandate.

**Stratification — these are the equity claim, so they are quotas, not hopes:**

| Stratum | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Regional / rural (MM 3–7) | ≥30% | The equity claim and the Innovation Victoria narrative |
| CALD / language other than English at home | ≥20% | Currently structural assertion only |
| First Nations | ≥5%, with cultural-safety oversight | Must not be tokenistic — see §9 |
| First baby | ~50% | Need profile differs sharply |
| Partners | ~25% of total | RQ6 |
| Antenatal / 0–6mo / 6–12mo | ~1/3 each | RQ1 stage variation |

**Target n.** Track A: 250–300 (indicative, quota-filled). Track B: 400+, powered for
subgroup comparison — final n set by the analysis plan, not by convenience.

## 6. Instrument structure

Target completion **12–14 minutes**. Beyond 15 minutes, completion collapses in this
population and the drop-off is not random — it is the most time-poor and most stressed,
who are exactly the people we most need.

| Module | Content | Approx. items |
|---|---|---|
| A. Screening & consent | Eligibility, consent, support resources shown up front | — |
| B. Context | Stage, parity, geography, language, care setting, supports | 8 |
| C. **Critical incidents** | Up to 3 recalled moments of unmet need: what, when, time of day, what you did, how long it took, how it resolved, how you felt | 12 |
| D. Current workarounds | What you use now, how much you trust it, what it fails at | 6 |
| E. **Trade-off core (MaxDiff)** | 12–16 support concepts, best-worst across ~10 screens | 10 screens |
| F. Modality in context | Three named scenarios (2am feed, first bath, a worry that won't settle) — what device, what format, what you'd actually do | 9 |
| G. AI acceptance | Expectations, concerns, conditions, what would make you stop; midwife supervision framing tested explicitly | 8 |
| H. Willingness & access | Who they think should pay; whether a service-provided offer changes uptake. **No price points** — see §12 | 4 |
| I. Follow-up consent | Opt-in to interview | 1 |

**Qualitative arm.** 20–25 semi-structured interviews, sampled purposively from Module I
opt-ins to over-represent the strata that are hardest to hear from. Interviews are where
RQ2 and RQ5 actually get answered; the survey tells you how common it is.

**Instrument development.** Seed the item pool from the existing content taxonomy and the
29 Aug evidence review, then run **8–10 cognitive interviews** before fielding. This step
is routinely skipped and is the difference between a usable instrument and a re-run.

## 7. Recruitment — and why it determines the ethics pathway

**Recruiting through a health service adds site research governance on top of HREC and can
add months.** Recruiting through community and consumer channels does not.

**Recommendation: recruit outside health services for this study.**

| Channel | Route | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Australian College of Midwives | Kelley Lennon / Gabby Earls, via Lois | Delivers the engagement we already owe them from April |
| Consumer organisations | e.g. peak perinatal consumer bodies | Also sources the consumer advisory seat |
| Community and social channels | Paid and organic | Cheapest quota-filling; weakest representativeness — watch it |
| Regional and CALD community orgs | Targeted, paid where appropriate | The only way the quotas in §5 actually fill |
| First Nations | **Only** via an appropriate partnering organisation | Never by open recruitment — see §9 |

**Incentive:** a modest, non-coercive gratuity (e.g. $20 voucher) plus a prize draw for the
survey; a larger gratuity for interviews. Required to fill the hard quotas; must be
justified to HREC as non-coercive.

## 8. Distress protocol — draft this now, not when HREC asks

Asking perinatal parents about unmet need, worry and difficult moments will surface
distress. This is foreseeable, so it is planned for.

1. **No diagnostic or screening instruments.** No EPDS, no PHQ. We are not screening and
   we must not appear to be — that is both an ethics issue and a SaMD-boundary issue.
2. **Support resources visible on every page**, not buried in a debrief — PANDA, Lifeline,
   13YARN, and the participant's own care provider.
3. **A stop rule for interviews**, with the interviewer trained in it and a named clinical
   contact for escalation on the day.
4. **No free-text field left unread.** A disclosure of risk in free text with no process to
   see it is worse than not asking.
5. **Anonymous by default** for the survey; identifiable only where a participant opts in
   to interview, and stored separately.

This section will be asked about. Having it drafted materially speeds approval.

## 9. Governance and cultural safety

- The study is the **first program under the new advisory committee** — it exercises the
  governance rather than waiting for it.
- **The consumer/lived-experience seat reviews the instrument before it is fielded.** We
  cannot run a study about what parents want whose design no parent has seen.
- **First Nations participation is not a quota to be filled by recruitment.** It requires
  a partnering organisation, appropriate review, and agreement on data governance
  (Indigenous Data Sovereignty). If that partnership is not in place, the honest position
  is to report the study as not including First Nations parents and say why — not to
  recruit a handful and imply coverage.
- **PhD firewall:** this study is designed to sit OUTSIDE Paper 5. If any part of it is
  intended to feed the thesis, that must be declared at the outset and the ethics pathway
  changes. Brad's dual role as CEO and Student Investigator is declared on the COI register
  and must be declared in the ethics application.
- **Brad does not conduct interviews.** The CEO interviewing prospective users about a
  product he sells is a coercion and bias problem that no amount of protocol language fixes.

## 10. Analysis plan

Pre-specified before data collection, and registered where the pathway allows.

- **Descriptive:** need frequency and severity by stage and stratum.
- **Critical incidents:** coded to the content taxonomy — this produces a **direct gap map
  against the RAG corpus**, which is the single most operationally useful output.
- **MaxDiff:** hierarchical Bayes utilities, overall and by stratum; report the rank order
  and where strata diverge.
- **Modality:** scenario-by-modality cross-tabs. The doctrine is confirmed or corrected on
  this table.
- **Qualitative:** reflexive thematic analysis, dual-coded, disagreements resolved by
  discussion.
- **Pre-commitment:** we state in advance which findings would cause us to change the
  product, and we publish the result whichever way it falls. A discovery study that could
  only ever confirm the roadmap was not worth running.

## 12. What this study deliberately does not do

- **No price testing.** Willingness-to-pay from consumers is meaningless in a model where a
  health service is the buyer, and asking risks anchoring a consumer-pays narrative that
  contradicts the pricing doctrine. Module H asks *who should pay*, not *how much*.
- **No clinical outcomes.** That is the pilot.
- **No efficacy claim of any kind.** Discovery only.
- **No product demonstration before the trade-off module.** Showing the Companion first
  turns preference elicitation into a reaction to our design.

## 14. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Track A determined to require HREC review, October data lost | Determine in week 1, not week 6. Plan the pack without it |
| Quotas do not fill on regional / CALD / First Nations | Budget for targeted paid recruitment from the start; report shortfalls honestly rather than weighting them away |

| Distress surfaced without a response path | §8, drafted before submission |
| The two tracks are conflated in the pack | Track A is never called a study, never cited as research, and is labelled indicative in every document |
